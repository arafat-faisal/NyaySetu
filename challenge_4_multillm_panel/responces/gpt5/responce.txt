PART 2: ARCHITECTURAL, LEGAL AND OPERATIONAL DEEP-DIVE
1. Statutory and ethical framework
Maintenance is suitable for mediation

The Family Courts Act 2023 places maintenance within Family Court jurisdiction under Section 5. However, the supplied materials incorrectly characterize Section 14 as the reconciliation provision. The official statute’s contents identify Section 11 as pre-trial proceedings, Section 14 as conclusion of trial, Section 15 as compromise decree, and Section 17 as enforcement. The final submission therefore avoids incorrectly attributing reconciliation or execution to Section 14.

Maintenance is suitable for ODR because the negotiation concerns lawful civil obligations capable of precise settlement:

monthly wife and child maintenance;
arrears and instalments;
payment dates and approved destination;
annual or event-based review;
proof of payment and default handling.

The Domestic Violence Act’s Section 11 permits an application by the victim or another person on her behalf. Sections 13 and 15 concern interim protection and residence orders. Section 16 is a compensation order, not simply a freestanding “maintenance section,” although monetary loss and appropriate financial relief may be addressed through it.

Correct treatment of the dowry allegation

A materially important correction is required: Section 7 of the Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 states that offences under the Act are cognizable, non-bailable and compoundable. Accordingly, a claim that Section 3 is legally “non-compoundable” would be vulnerable before legally trained judges. Section 3 nevertheless makes demanding dowry an offence punishable by imprisonment, fine, or both.

The stronger winning position is therefore:

The dowry allegation is not sent to online mediation in this case because violence, surveillance, retaliation risk and lack of confidential, voluntary participation make ODR unsafe and ethically defective, not because the Act invariably labels the offence non-compoundable.

Even where compromise may legally be possible, the platform must not facilitate payment, waiver, suppression of evidence or coercive settlement of an alleged offence. The motorcycle demand must never be converted into a negotiable term. The system preserves the allegation and refers it for human legal assessment.

2. Low-bandwidth asynchronous caucus design

The uploaded case establishes that Moyuri has only a short safe speaking window, Sohel works shifts in Gazipur, and Ripon cannot use visual interfaces. The minimum architecture therefore uses the ordinary telephone network, assisted UDC access and separate audio caucuses rather than video conferencing.

The mediator operates a controlled shuttle process:

A female officer privately hears Moyuri during the registered safe window.
The officer records only a structured summary necessary for mediation.
The officer separately contacts Sohel during an agreed break.
Offers are relayed without exposing telephone numbers or opening direct messaging.
Each caucus receives a session identifier, start and end time, participant-verification method and mediator note.
If the call drops, the session pauses without treating silence as consent.
A settlement is produced only after both parties separately hear identical terms.

“Asynchronous” must not mean unattended AI negotiation. AI may assist transcription, translation, redaction or scheduling, but a human officer must verify outputs and make every eligibility, safety, referral and settlement-recording decision.

The file should contain separate fields for “contactable number,” “safe recipient,” “safe time,” “safe channel” and “prohibited channel.” This prevents an ordinary automated notification from becoming a source of retaliation.

4. Enforceability and MFS accountability

The currently operative reform is the Legal Aid Services (Amendment) Act 2026, Act No. 45 of 2026, dated 10 April 2026. It replaced the 2025 ordinance and inserted Sections 21B, 21C, 21D and 21E into the principal Act. Some provisions concerning mandatory pre-case mediation and the statutory schedule depend on government notification specifying the date and area of operation.

Consequently, the design should not casually promise “automatic conversion under Section 21A.” The production system must maintain a jurisdiction-and-commencement rules table that checks:

the applicable legal-aid office;
whether the relevant provision has commenced in Joypurhat;
the dispute category;
the authorized officer or mediator;
execution formalities;
the legally prescribed enforcement route.

MFS integration should be an accountability ledger, not unlicensed escrow. With informed consent, the system records the provider, transaction reference, amount, due date and payer confirmation. It should not expose Moyuri’s location or wallet information to Sohel beyond what payment law requires.

A rules engine may flag:

payment not matched by the due date;
partial payment;
repeated late payment;
inconsistent payer identity;
possible reversal or duplicate reference.

A human officer then verifies the event, contacts Moyuri only through the safe channel and decides whether to issue a notice, seek execution, vary arrangements or provide further legal aid. Auto-preparing documents is appropriate; automatically filing proceedings without legal review, verified default and client instructions is not.

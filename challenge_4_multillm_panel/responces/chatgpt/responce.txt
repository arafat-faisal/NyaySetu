The ODR record is locked from joint contact; the DLAO/legal-aid team instead routes urgent protective relief under Domestic Violence Act 2010, including Section 13 interim protection and Section 15 residence relief, and refers the dowry-demand offence under Section 4 of the Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 to the competent criminal court/police pathway. A local female legal-aid worker is dispatched for safe in-person contact. AI may transcribe or flag missing fields, but never decides eligibility, voluntariness, safety, settlement, or referral. Every material action is time-stamped and attributable to a named officer, preserving confidentiality, auditability and appeal/complaint review. ## PART 2: ARCHITECTURAL, LEGAL & OPERATIONAL DEEP-DIVE

### 1. Statutory and ethical framework

The strongest legal design is actually narrower than the supplied attachment. The Family Courts Act 2023 places maintenance within Family Court jurisdiction and expressly requires reconciliation efforts in proceedings.  The CPC likewise provides the institutional mediation architecture through Section 89A and expressly allows referral to the Legal Aid Officer. 

The important correction is the Dowry Prohibition Act 2018. Its Section 3 concerns giving, taking, or abetting dowry; **direct or indirect demand is Section 4**. More importantly, the currently published text states in Section 8 that offences under that Act are non-cognizable, non-bailable and compoundable.  Therefore, the submission should **not** claim that Section 3 makes the demand universally non-compoundable. The safer legal position is: the system never treats the dowry demand as a bargaining term, and in this fact pattern the active violence, threats and coercive environment independently require mediation refusal.

That distinction is critical. A technologically impressive system that falsely characterizes a statute will be weaker than a system demonstrating statutory precision.

The Domestic Violence Act supports the safety gate. Section 13 provides interim protection orders and Section 15 residence orders; Section 16 concerns compensation rather than simply being a generic “maintenance” provision. ([Advocatetanmoy Law Library][1])

### 2. Low-bandwidth asynchronous caucus

The core architecture should be PSTN/IVR-first, not video-first. Bangladesh's national legal-aid helpline 16699 is already functioning as a major access channel, with NLASO reporting substantial use through the helpline in 2026. ([BSS][2])

The operational unit is a **voice case**, not an app session:

**16699/UDC intake -> human screening -> separate audio caucuses -> structured settlement record -> judicial review.**

This solves the geographic and scheduling asymmetry:

* Moyuri does not need continuous connectivity. Her information can be gathered in short, scheduled voice windows and cached server-side.
* Sohel can be contacted at a scheduled factory break rather than requiring simultaneous attendance.
* The mediator controls the shuttle, so neither party needs to manage a conference platform.
* UDC staff provide the physical bridge for identity verification, assisted form completion and witnessed execution when safe.

Asynchronous does not mean unattended. Every substantive communication remains attributable to the mediator and logged as an official case event.

### 3. Zero-screen accessibility and safety

Ripon should be treated as a **voice-assisted representative**, not as a substitute decision-maker. His report can initiate intake, but Moyuri's own consent must be independently confirmed whenever a decision affects her rights.

For Ripon, every critical output should have a voice equivalent: case status, appointment, requested information, draft settlement and payment confirmation. Captcha-heavy workflows, PDF-first notices and visual OTP dependence should be eliminated.

Moyuri's security model is even more important. The system should maintain a **safe-contact profile** containing only the minimum data needed to prevent accidental disclosure. No unsolicited SMS should be generated to her surveilled handset. The system should support neutral outbound calling patterns, scheduled callback windows, code words where appropriate, and rapid suppression of all party-to-party communications after a safety flag.

AI is useful only at the support layer: transcription, Bangla voice-to-text indexing, duplicate detection and missing-field prompts. It must never make the safety or mediation eligibility decision.

### 4. Enforceability and accountability

The digital record should be structured as an evidentiary chain:

**Intake -> identity/representation record -> consent event -> each caucus event -> settlement version -> execution event -> DLAO certification -> payment events -> default review.**

Do not describe the MFS component as a magical “escrow” unless the actual payment architecture legally supports escrow. A more defensible design is a **case-linked payment ledger** integrating permitted MFS rails or verified payment references.

Likewise, avoid claiming that the system can automatically attach wages merely because a payment is late. Default should create an **execution/referral task for a human legal officer or court process**, not an automatic coercive remedy.

The central accountability feature is therefore not automation; it is **traceability**. Every material decision has an officer, timestamp, source record and status. That directly implements the hackathon's human-in-the-loop principle.

Finally, current implementation confirms that Bangladesh is actively expanding pre-case mediation under the 2026 reforms, including rollout in Joypurhat and other districts, making a safety-first digital intake and mediation layer operationally timely rather than merely theoretical. ([Jagonews24][3])

**Bottom line:** the winning architecture is not “mediate everything online.” It is **mediate only the legally and ethically suitable issue, through voice-first infrastructure, while making victim safety a hard judicial gate and preserving an immediate non-ODR pathway when that gate fails.**

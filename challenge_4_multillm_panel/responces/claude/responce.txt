## PART 2: ARCHITECTURAL, LEGAL & OPERATIONAL DEEP-DIVE

**1. Statutory and ethical framework — why the two claims split.** The maintenance claim is a civil, quantifiable, ongoing-relationship obligation (Family Courts Act 2023 Sections 4–5; Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 Section 9; DV Act 2010 Section 16) — the kind of dispute mediation exists to resolve, since a monthly figure and a payment channel are legitimately negotiable. The dowry demand is a different category entirely: Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 Section 3 makes it a cognizable, non-bailable criminal offense, and Bangladesh ADR doctrine (consistent with UNODC restorative-justice standards) treats criminal coercion as non-compoundable precisely because "mediating" it would let the offender bargain down a crime with the victim who has no real bargaining power. Bundling both claims into one mediation would let a real threat (the motorcycle demand) quietly discount a legal entitlement (maintenance) — so statutory design, not just caution, requires the split.

**2. Low-bandwidth asynchronous caucus design.** Video is ruled out by both parties' constraints, not just preference: Sohel's factory shifts leave no continuous free block, and Moyuri's 2G connection drops for hours. A shuttle (caucus) model over PSTN voice — the mediator speaking to each side separately and on their own schedule — tolerates Moyuri's short windows and Sohel's shift breaks without requiring simultaneity, and it removes the risk of the parties confronting each other live, which matters given the documented history of battery.

**3. Zero-screen accessibility and safety protocol.** Every citizen-facing touchpoint (entry, screening, consent) is voice-based specifically because Ripon cannot process text, captchas, or OTPs, and Moyuri has never used the internet.

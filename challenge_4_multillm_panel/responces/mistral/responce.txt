## **PART 2: ARCHITECTURAL, LEGAL & OPERATIONAL DEEP-DIVE**

### **1. Statutory & Ethical Framework: Compoundable vs. Non-Compoundable Claims**

**Maintenance Claim (Mediatable):**
- **Legal Basis**: Family Courts Act 2023 Section 5 (wife and minor child maintenance), Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 Section 9 (husband's maintenance obligation), Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 16 (monetary relief).
- **Mediation Viability**: Maintenance is a **civil, compoundable** financial dispute. Parties can lawfully negotiate and settle monetary terms (monthly allowance, child support, payment schedules) through mediation. Under Legal Aid Services Act 2000 Section 21A(4) and CPC Section 89A, mediated settlements achieve **full decree status**, enforceable as a Civil Court judgment without further trial.
- **Ethical Justification**: Maintenance negotiations involve quantifiable financial obligations where both parties retain agency over terms. Asynchronous audio mediation eliminates power asymmetry by removing direct confrontation and allowing separate, safe participation.

**Dowry Demand (Non-Mediatable):**
- **Legal Basis**: Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 Section 3 (cognizable, non-bailable criminal offense), Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 3 (economic abuse), Penal Code 1860 Sections 323/506 (criminal intimidation).
- **Mediation Viability**: **Zero**. Bangladesh criminal jurisprudence and Supreme Court precedent **explicitly prohibit** mediation for non-compoundable offenses. Dowry extortion is a **public wrong** against the state; private settlement would condone criminal coercion and endanger the victim.
- **Ethical Imperative**: Moyuri lacks **free, uncoerced consent** due to:
  - Physical surveillance (mother-in-law controls button phone, checks call logs)
  - Restricted mobility (cannot travel alone; NID locked away)
  - Active threats (battery, second marriage ultimatum)
  - **Power Asymmetry**: Any "negotiation" under these conditions is inherently coercive.

### **2. Low-Bandwidth Asynchronous Caucus Design**

**Why Video Conferencing Fails:**
- **Technical**: Gazipur factory floors and Kalai rural areas suffer **2G instability, frequent drops, and low bandwidth**. Video buffering (WebRTC/Zoom) is impractical.
- **Cultural/Accessibility**: Sohel is uncomfortable with complex apps; Moyuri has **zero internet literacy**; Ripon cannot interact with visual interfaces.
- **Safety**: Joint video calls risk **emotional bullying** and **retaliation** if Sohel perceives Moyuri's participation.

**Asynchronous Tele-Caucus Solution:**
- **Shuttle Mediation**: Mediator conducts **separate, non-simultaneous audio calls**—no joint sessions.
  - **Sohel**: Scheduled during Gazipur factory breaks (lunch/evening) on his smartphone via IVR.
  - **Moyuri**: Conducted at Kalai UDC with a **female paralegal** during her Friday 12:45–1:15 PM safe window, using a landline or UDC phone.
- **Network Resilience**: Standard cellular voice (PSTN) operates on **8–32 kbps**, unaffected by data drops. Calls can be **retried, recorded, and transcribed** asynchronously.
- **Cultural Fit**: Voice-first aligns with Bangladesh's **oral legal tradition** and high mobile penetration (98%+).

**Issue Narrowing:**
- **Exclusive Focus on Maintenance**: The mediator **strictly confines** discussions to quantifiable maintenance (BDT 4,000/month, child support, payment dates). Dowry demands are **explicitly excluded** from mediation scope.

### **4. Enforceability & MFS Accountability**

**Judicial Certification:**
- DLAO (Senior Assistant Judge) reviews the digital settlement record (audio + UDC thumbprint scan) and applies a **digital judicial signature**.
- Under Legal Aid Services Act 2000 **Section 21A(4)**, the settlement carries the **full force of a Civil Court decree**.
- **CPC Section 89A** confirms enforceability: mediated settlements are **executable as decrees** without fresh evidentiary trials.

**Mobile Financial Services (MFS) Integration:**
- **Case-ID Linked Payments**: Sohel remits maintenance via bKash/Nagad using his **DLAS case ID** (e.g., `DLAS-JP-2026-0042`). The system:
  1. **Validates** the case ID against the settlement record.
  2. **Disburses** funds to Moyuri's mother's **verified MFS wallet** (pre-registered during intake).
  3. **Logs** transactions in an **immutable digital ledger** (timestamp, amount, parties).

**Automated Accountability:**
- **Silent Verification**: Ripon receives an **automated voice call**: *"Monthly maintenance of BDT 4,000 deposited successfully to [mother's number]."* No SMS (risky for Moyuri).
- **Default Escalation**:
  - **10-Day Grace Period**: System sends automated **warning call** to Sohel.
  - **30-Day Default**: System **automatically files** an Execution Petition (Jari Mamla) in Joypurhat Family Court under **Family Courts Act 2023 Section 14**.
  - **Attachment**: Sohel's Gazipur wages are **automatically attached** via employer coordination (pre-authorized during mediation).
  - **Zero Victim Burden**: Moyuri **never** needs to travel, hire a lawyer, or appear in court.

**Legal Backing for Automation:**
- **Section 21A(4)**: DLAO's certification enables **direct execution** of mediated settlements.
- **Family Courts Act Section 14**: Empowers courts to enforce maintenance decrees via wage attachment.
- **Digital Evidence Act 2023**: Recognizes **digital records** (call logs, MFS transactions) as admissible evidence.

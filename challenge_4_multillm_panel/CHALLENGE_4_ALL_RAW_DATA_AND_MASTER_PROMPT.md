### 1. STATUTORY FOUNDATION OF PRE-CASE MEDIATION IN BANGLADESH

In Bangladesh, legal aid mediation has evolved from an informal alternative into an institutionally backed, statutory dispute resolution mechanism.

1. **Legal Aid Services Act 2000 (Amended 2026)**:
   - **Section 21A (Pre-Case and Post-Filing Mediation)**: Empowers the District Legal Aid Officer (DLAO)—who is a serving judicial officer (Senior Assistant Judge or Joint District Judge) on deputation—to conduct pre-case mediation (before formal court litigation) as well as referral mediation from pending civil/family courts.
   - **Section 21A(4) (Enforceability of Mediated Settlement)**: Any settlement agreement executed before the Legal Aid Officer has the legal force and effect of a decree passed by a civil court under Section 89A of the Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC). It is directly executable in court without fresh evidentiary trial if either party defaults.
   - **Section 7**: Mandates DBLA to establish digital infrastructure, remote access facilities, and secure records for legal aid service delivery.

2. **Code of Civil Procedure 1908 (CPC)**:
   - **Section 89A & 89B**: Institutionalized mediation and arbitration in civil litigation. Mediation settlements recorded in prescribed form become enforceable court decrees with no right of appeal, barring fraud.

3. **Family Courts Act 2023**:
   - **Sections 4 & 5**: Vests exclusive jurisdiction over five family matters: dissolution of marriage, restitution of conjugal rights, dower, maintenance, and guardianship/custody of children.
   - **Section 14 (Compulsory Pre-Trial & Post-Trial Reconciliation/Mediation)**: The Family Court is statutorily required to hold informal, camera reconciliation sessions between the parties. Pre-case mediation conducted under DBLA Section 21A directly aligns with and satisfies this policy.

---

### 2. SUBSTANTIVE CLAIMS: MAINTENANCE VS. DOWRY EXTORTION

Challenge 4 involves two fundamentally different legal subject matters requiring careful legal distinction:

#### A. The Maintenance Claim (Legally Compoundable & Mediatable)
- **Legal Grounds**:
  - *Family Courts Act 2023, Section 5*: Wife and minor child's statutory entitlement to reasonable maintenance (*nafafa* / *khorkhurchi*).
  - *Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961, Section 9*: Obligation of the husband to adequately maintain his wife and children.
  - *Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2010, Section 16*: Jurisdiction to grant monetary compensation, living expenses, medical relief, and child support.
- **Mediation Viability**: High. Setting monetary maintenance, child support schedules, bank/MFS transfer accounts, and arrears payment timelines is a classic, compoundable civil dispute well-suited to ADR/ODR.

#### B. The Dowry Demand (Strict Non-Compoundable Criminal Offense)
- **Legal Grounds**:
  - *Dowry Prohibition Act 2018, Section 3*: Demanding dowry directly or indirectly (here, demanding a motorcycle backed by threats of second marriage and abandonment) is a cognizable, non-bailable criminal offense punishable by up to 5 years imprisonment and a fine.
  - *Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2010, Section 3*: Economic abuse, coercive dowry demands, and nighttime domestic battery constitute domestic violence.
  - *Penal Code 1860, Sections 323, 325, and 506*: Causing hurt, grievous hurt, and criminal intimidation.
- **Mediation Viability**: **ZERO / STATUTORILY BARRED**. In Bangladesh criminal jurisprudence and Supreme Court doctrine, criminal offenses involving domestic cruelty, non-compoundable dowry extortion, and physical violence cannot be bargained away, settled, or compromised through mediation. Doing so condones criminal coercion and endangers the victim.

### 3. THE MANDATORY MEDIATION HALT & EMERGENCY EXIT PROTOCOL

Under national and international dispute resolution standards (including UNODC standards on restorative justice and domestic abuse, and Bangladesh National Legal Aid Guidelines):

#### When Must Online Mediation Stop and Refuse to Proceed?
Online mediation **MUST STOP AND BE REFUSED** when:
> **The screening detects active domestic battery, ongoing threats of violence, criminal dowry extortion, or severe power asymmetry / duress where the victim lacks uncoerced agency, physical safety, or an independent confidential space.**

#### Legal & Operational Rationale for Halting:
1. **Lack of Voluntariness & Free Consent**: Under contract law and ADR ethics, mediation requires free, uncoerced consent. A woman living in her husband's family home, surveilled by in-laws, whose NID is locked away and who can speak for only 20 minutes a week, cannot negotiate freely online.
2. **Danger of Retaliatory Violence**: Engaging Sohel in an online dispute while Moyuri is trapped in his family's rural house puts her at extreme risk of immediate physical battery.
3. **Compounding Non-Compoundable Crime**: A legal aid mediator cannot legally negotiate a discount on an illegal dowry demand.

#### What Must the System Do Instead?
When online mediation is refused, the DLAS system must execute an **Emergency Judicial Referral & Protection Protocol**:
1. **Immediate Pipeline Shift**: Automatically close the ODR track, lock the case file against joint party contact, and mark the matter as `CRITICAL: DOMESTIC VIOLENCE & DOWRY - JUDICIAL INTERVENTION ONLY`.
2. **Ex-Parte Judicial Application**: Escalate the file directly to the District Legal Aid Officer (Senior Assistant Judge) to initiate urgent court proceedings under the Domestic Violence Act 2010:
   - **Section 13**: Application for an ex-parte Interim Protection Order restraining Sohel and his family from committing acts of domestic violence, entering her living space, or contacting her.
   - **Section 15**: Residence Order securing Moyuri's right to live peacefully in the shared household or directing alternative accommodation/maintenance.
3. **Statutory Notice to Enforcement Officer**: Notify the Upazila Women Affairs Officer (designated Enforcement Officer under Section 5 of the DV Act 2010) and local Police Station (Thana) for safety monitoring and shelter assistance.
4. **Criminal Filing Referral**: Route the criminal complaint under Section 3 of the Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 to the Senior Judicial Magistrate Court Joypurhat for criminal cognizance.
5. **Local Physical Safeguard**: Dispatch a female community paralegal or Union Parishad Legal Aid volunteer to establish safe, in-person contact with Moyuri during her Friday window or at her mother's home.

### 3. DETAILED STAGE-BY-STAGE FUNCTIONALITY

#### Stage 1: Citizen Entry (Zero-Screen Voice Gateway)
- **Mechanism**: Ripon dials 16699 or visits the local Kalai Union Digital Center (UDC). An IVR voice agent or UDC entrepreneur initiates a voice-guided ticket.

### SECTION 2: JURISPRUDENTIAL & ARCHITECTURAL ANALYSIS

#### 1. Statutory Grounding of Pre-Case Mediation vs. Criminal Dowry
- **Maintenance Compoundability**: Under Family Courts Act 2023 Section 5 and Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 16, maintenance claims are civil in character and can be lawfully compromised.

## 1. Statutory & Ethical Framework: Why Maintenance is Mediatable While Dowry is Barred

### The Compoundable Civil Claim

The maintenance claim operates within a well-established statutory framework designed to facilitate amicable resolution. Under Family Courts Act 2023 Section 5, a wife and minor child possess statutory entitlement to reasonable maintenance. The Muslim Family Laws Ordinance 1961 Section 9 reinforces the husband's obligation. Crucially, the Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 16 vests jurisdiction to grant monetary compensation, living expenses, and child support.

These provisions create what is legally classified as a compoundable civil dispute. The subject matter concerns quantifiable financial obligations capable of negotiation without compromising public policy or enabling criminal conduct. The Code of Civil Procedure 1908 Sections 89A and 89B institutionalized mediation precisely for such matters, with settlements recorded in prescribed form becoming enforceable court decrees with no right of appeal barring fraud.

Section 21A of the Legal Aid Services Act 2000, as amended in 2026, empowers the District Legal Aid Officer to conduct pre-case mediation. Critically, Section 21A(4) provides that any settlement executed before the Legal Aid Officer carries the legal force and effect of a civil court decree under CPC Section 89A. This statutory architecture demonstrates legislative intent that family financial disputes should be resolved through structured mediation before formal litigation.

The ethical foundation for mediating maintenance is equally sound. UNODC standards on restorative justice recognize that financial disputes between parties with ongoing relationships benefit from consensual resolution processes that preserve dignity and enable future compliance. For Moyuri, mediation offers tangible advantages: no court appearance, no travel to Joypurhat, no adversarial confrontation, and the possibility of receiving regular financial support without prolonged litigation.

### The Non-Compoundable Criminal Offense

The dowry demand, by contrast, involves fundamentally different legal character. Under Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 Section 3, demanding dowry directly or indirectly constitutes a cognizable, non-bailable criminal offense punishable by up to five years imprisonment and fine. The specific conduct alleged, demanding a motorcycle backed by threats of second marriage and abandonment while simultaneously subjecting Moyuri to nighttime battery, falls squarely within this prohibition.

The Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 3 further characterizes economic abuse, coercive dowry demands, and domestic battery as domestic violence. These offenses are classified as non-compoundable under Bangladesh criminal jurisprudence and Supreme Court doctrine. Non-compoundable offenses cannot be bargained away, settled, or compromised through mediation because doing so would condone criminal coercion, undermine public policy, and endanger the victim.

The ethical prohibition is absolute. A legal aid mediator cannot legally negotiate a discount on an illegal dowry demand. The mediator's statutory role under Section 21A does not extend to authorizing criminal conduct or permitting a perpetrator to purchase immunity from prosecution. Permitting mediation of the dowry demand would transform the legal aid system into an instrument of extortion, allowing Sohel to continue his coercive demands while enjoying protection from criminal liability.

The Supreme Court of Bangladesh has consistently held that domestic violence and dowry-related offenses cannot be mediated because the power asymmetry between perpetrator and victim prevents genuinely voluntary consent.

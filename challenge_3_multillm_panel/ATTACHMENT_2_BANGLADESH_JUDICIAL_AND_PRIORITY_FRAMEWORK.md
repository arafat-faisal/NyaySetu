# ATTACHMENT 2: BANGLADESH JUDICIAL JURISPRUDENCE & STATUTORY TRIAGE FRAMEWORK

This document details the judicial standards, statutory mandates, and case management principles governing the District Legal Aid Officer (DLAO) in Joypurhat under Bangladesh law.

---

## 1. STATUTORY MANDATE OF THE DISTRICT LEGAL AID OFFICER (DLAO)

- **Judicial Status**: Under Section 21A of the **Legal Aid Services Act, 2000 (as amended in 2026)**, the District Legal Aid Officer (DLAO) is a serving judicial officer (Senior Assistant Judge or Joint District Judge) appointed from the Bangladesh Judicial Service on judicial deputation.
- **Non-Delegation Doctrine**:
  - Under Bangladesh constitutional jurisprudence and judicial service rules, the exercise of judicial discretion—such as assessing case merit, determining legal aid entitlement, granting interim relief, or issuing notices—cannot be delegated to automated algorithms or artificial intelligence.
  - Technology may only assist in data organization, sorting, and decision-support, while all substantive legal determinations remain exclusively human-centric.
- **Mandatory Pre-Case Mediation Mandate**:
  - In Joypurhat district, government notifications issued under the 2026 amendments enforce pre-case mediation for specific civil and familial disputes. However, where active domestic violence or immediate bodily peril is established, emergency protective remedies under the Domestic Violence Act take precedence over mediation.

---

## 2. STATUTORY PRIORITY CATEGORIES (LEGAL AID SERVICES POLICY 2014)

Paragraph 2 of the **Legal Aid Services Policy, 2014** establishes statutory categories that are entitled to priority legal assistance:
1. Insolvent women and children;
2. Victims of domestic violence, human trafficking, acid attack, or sexual violence;
3. Persons with physical, visual, or mental disabilities (expressly qualifying Ripon under Clause 2(n));
4. Undertrial prisoners detained in jail custody without legal counsel;
5. Any person incapable of self-defense due to extreme poverty, homelessness, or natural disaster.

## 3. STATUTORY BASES FOR THE THREE OPERATIONAL TRIAGE FACTORS

When reviewing an incoming docket of cases, the DLAO must balance multiple statutory and humanitarian pressures:

### Factor 1: Imminent Threat to Life, Physical Integrity & Minor Welfare
- **Statutory Anchors**:
  - *Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act, 2010 (Sections 3 & 13)*: Power to issue urgent ex parte Interim Protection Orders where domestic violence is occurring or likely to recur.
  - *Penal Code, 1860 (Sections 323, 325, 342)*: Hurt, grievous hurt, and wrongful confinement.
  - *Child Endangerment*: Five-month total denial of food and infant maintenance triggers emergency relief under Section 16 of the DV Act.

### Factor 2: Extreme Vulnerability, Isolation & Communication Quarantine
- **Statutory Anchors**:
  - *Legal Aid Services Policy, 2014 (Paragraph 2(a) & 2(n))*: Compounded vulnerability (illiterate/rural woman + infant dependent + blind proxy caller).
  - *Anti-Surveillance Imperative*: A monitored household button phone and locked NID create a unique risk profile where conventional digital or mail outreach will directly endanger the applicant (violating Article 32 of the Constitution).

### Factor 3: Statutory Legal Merit & Irremediable Temporal Harm
- **Statutory Anchors**:
  - *Dowry Prohibition Act, 2018 (Section 3)*: Cognizable, non-bailable offense of demanding dowry backed by coercive threats of second marriage and desertion.
  - *Domestic Violence Act, 2010 (Section 15)*: Imminent risk of unlawful eviction from the marital residence.
  - *Peremptory Deadlines*: Coercive deadlines (e.g., threat to marry again if motorcycle is not provided) create a risk of irremediable legal and personal injury if court intervention is delayed.

## 4. PROCEDURAL SAFEGUARDS AGAINST ALGORITHMIC BIAS

Under the principles of Natural Justice and Article 27 of the Constitution of Bangladesh (equality before law):
1. **The Incomplete-Data Penalty Trap**: Standard administrative algorithms unfairly deprioritize files that lack complete fields (e.g., missing NID number, lack of uploaded documentary evidence, unverified phone number). In Bangladesh's rural legal-aid context, incomplete data is almost always an indicator of **deep structural vulnerability**, not lack of merit.
2. **Mandatory Inverse-Uncertainty Safeguard**: The digital system must treat data deficits from marginalized callers not as reasons to downgrade a case, but as **triggers for expedited human investigative outreach**.

### 2. Zero-Harm Anti-Surveillance Safeguards

The case presents a lethal surveillance environment where a single mistimed digital touchpoint can trigger severe retaliatory violence. The following safeguards are mandatory:

**A. Absolute Prohibition on Automated Outbound Contact**
Under the "Do-No-Harm" Directive, the system must permanently block all automated SMS, standard callback IVR, or postal letters to Moyuri's household. An SMS to the shared button phone will be inspected by the mother-in-law or Sohel, directly endangering Moyuri. The AI must record an explicit, hard-coded warning in the dossier: "DO NOT SEND SMS OR OUTBOUND CALL TO RESIDENCE BUTTON PHONE."

**B. Temporal Safe-Window Mapping**
The AI must capture and flag the only known safe communication window: approximately 20 minutes on Friday midday while Sohel is at Jumu'ah prayer and the mother-in-law rests. This temporal intelligence allows the human DLAO to plan confidential victim contact without exposing Moyuri.

**C. Covert Offline Verification via Statutory Protection Officers**
Given that Moyuri cannot travel 30 km to the Joypurhat District Legal Aid Office and cannot safely receive calls, the DLAO must trigger an offline, in-person verification mechanism. Under Section 14 of the Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2010, the Upazila Women Affairs Officer (Protection Officer) has a statutory mandate to intervene. A female Protection Officer, female Union Parishad member, or trusted community para-legal can visit the residence under an innocuous welfare or health cover (e.g., maternal-child health check, routine Union Parishad welfare visit) to establish direct, confidential contact with Moyuri and confirm her consent and factual claims. This bypasses the surveilled phone entirely.

**D. Constitutional and Penal Code Imperatives**
Disclosing legal aid outreach to the abusive husband violates Moyuri's constitutional right to life and personal liberty under Article 32 of the Constitution of Bangladesh. It also risks aggravating offenses under Sections 323 (voluntarily causing hurt) and 325 (grievous hurt) of the Penal Code 1860. The AI must therefore treat surveillance mitigation as a non-negotiable system constraint, not an optional preference.

### 3. Accessibility Accommodations for Visually Impaired Proxy

Ripon's disability profile imposes strict accessibility requirements on the DLAS architecture:

**A. 100% Voice-Driven Interaction**
The entire intake must operate through natural spoken Bangla. The system cannot require Ripon to read text, PDFs, forms, or graphical interfaces. No visual CAPTCHA or screen-based confirmation may be requested.

**B. No SMS/OTP Dead-Ends**
Because Ripon cannot read Bangla text and screen readers fail on Bengali labels, SMS-based OTPs or text confirmations are inaccessible and must be excluded. Authentication and confirmation must rely on spoken tokens (e.g., a memorizable 4-digit verbal reference code repeated audibly at the end of the call).

**C. Auditory Confirmation & Reference Codes**
At the conclusion of the intake, the AI must provide a clear, spoken reference number that Ripon can memorize and repeat to the DLAO during any follow-up. All status updates must be delivered via return voice calls, not text messages.

**D. Calibrated Pacing**
The system must accommodate natural speech pauses and regional Bangla phrasing, allowing Ripon to narrate facts in his own temporal and episodic structure without forcing rigid form-field timing that could disorient a blind caller relying on auditory memory.

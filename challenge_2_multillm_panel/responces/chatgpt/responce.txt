### 2. Zero-Harm / Anti-Surveillance Design

The system should adopt a **“no unsolicited outbound contact”** state as soon as Ripon reports that household communications are monitored.

The following should be technically blocked, not merely discouraged:

* SMS to the button phone.
* Automated callback or IVR to the household.
* Verification links or app invitations.
* Voice messages naming “legal aid,” “domestic violence,” “DLAO,” or “Moyuri.”
* Any automated communication with Sohel.
* Any notification that could expose the existence of the case.

The safe Friday window should be stored as a **restricted human-contact constraint**, not as an automated appointment. The DLAO or designated officer determines whether contact is actually safe on that occasion.

The strongest statutory route for covert verification is the Domestic Violence Act's **Enforcement Officer** mechanism. Under section 5, the Enforcement Officer may be appointed for the relevant upazila/thana/district; the Act defines that officer as the Upazila Women Affairs Officer or another government-appointed officer. Section 6 assigns duties including assisting the victim to obtain legal aid, assisting with applications for protection orders upon request, maintaining service-provider links, and arranging shelter according to the victim's consent. ([BD Laws][2])

Thus, the system should not invent a covert "AI investigation." It should create a human task such as:

> **“Extreme surveillance hazard. Do not contact household electronically. Consider confidential in-person verification through the Enforcement Officer or another lawful, safe channel.”**

### 3. Accessibility for Ripon

The proxy interaction should remain entirely auditory:

**Speak → listen → confirm → continue.**

No captcha, PDF, form, visual screen, Bengali screen-reader dependency, SMS OTP, or web link should be required.

The system can confirm extracted facts conversationally:

> “আপনি কি বলছেন, আপনার বোন কালাই, জয়পুরহাটে থাকেন এবং তাঁর চার বছরের একটি মেয়ে আছে?”

A simple spoken reference code can be used for continuity, but it must never become a barrier to human escalation.

The distinction between accessibility and authority is crucial: Ripon's inability to read or see must **not** reduce the system's willingness to capture and escalate his report. His disability is an accessibility fact, not a reason for rejection.

### 6. Critical Correction to the Supplied Statutory Notes

For a competition submission, I strongly recommend **not repeating the section numbering in the supplied Attachment 2**, because several citations there are materially inaccurate.

The official Bangladesh text shows:

* **DV Act §10 = right to reside in shared residence**, not interim protection.
* **§11 = application to court**.
* **§12 = place of filing**.
* **§13 = interim protection order**.
* **§14 = protection order**.
* **§15 = residence order**.
* **§16 = compensation/maintenance**. ([BD Laws][2])

Also, the Act does **not** put the third-party application rule in §16; the relevant authority is **§11(1)**. The Enforcement Officer mechanism is grounded in **§§5–6**. ([BD Laws][2])

Similarly, under the Dowry Prohibition Act 2018, **§3 is the provision on demanding dowry**, while **§4 concerns giving/receiving/assisting/agreement to give or receive dowry**. 

Finally, the 2026 Legal Aid amendment is real and currently reflected in the official Bangladesh Laws database. The amended law expressly includes emergency legal support, referral mechanisms, digital accessibility/data security, and Legal Aid Officer functions. 

One particularly important current point for the hackathon: the 2026 amendment's mandatory pre-case mediation provisions have been brought into force in **Joypurhat from 31 July 2026**, and the schedule includes complaints under **Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 §§3–4**. Therefore, a human officer should determine the appropriate procedural route; the AI should never automatically decide that mediation, litigation, police action, or any other remedy is legally required. 

The official NLASO site also confirms **16699** as the current National Legal Aid Helpline. ([nlaso.gov.bd][3])

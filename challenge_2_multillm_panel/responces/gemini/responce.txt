### SECTION 2: ARCHITECTURAL, SAFETY & STATUTORY RATIONALE

To ensure complete clarity on the technological and legal mechanics of this solution, the following is a comprehensive, step-by-step architectural and statutory breakdown of the proposed Digital Legal Aid System (DLAS).

#### 1. Decision Workflow Diagram / Step-by-Step Flow

The DLAS architecture relies on an asynchronous, highly secure intake pipeline designed specifically for proxy reporting.

* **Step 1: Inbound Accessibility Routing.** Ripon dials 16699. The system recognizes standard feature-phone audio and immediately routes to the Natural Language Understanding (NLU) module optimized for colloquial spoken Bangla.


* **Step 2: Empathetic Entity Extraction.** Through conversational prompts, the AI extracts structured variables from unstructured dialogue: Victim Identity (Moyuri), Perpetrator Identity (Sohel), Geography (Kalai Upazila), and Abuse Metrics (physical battery, starvation, dowry extortion).


* **Step 3: Hazard & Triage Classification.** The NLU detects keywords related to phone confiscation and visual impairment. The system immediately classifies the caller as a "Disabled Proxy" and the victim under an "Extreme Surveillance Hazard".


* **Step 4: Outbound Communications Lockdown.** A hard-coded kill switch activates, disabling all automated SMS receipts or standard follow-up calls to both Ripon (who cannot read them) and Moyuri (who would be endangered by them).


* **Step 5: DLAO Handoff.** The AI generates a "Third-Party Intake Packet," assigns a 4-digit spoken verbal code to Ripon, and queues the dossier for an immediate high-priority human review by the Joypurhat District Legal Aid Officer.



#### 2. Zero-Harm Anti-Surveillance Safeguards

The domestic surveillance dynamic is the most critical threat vector in this case. Any technological footprint left by the DLAS could trigger severe retaliatory domestic violence under Sections 323, 325, and 342 of the Penal Code 1860.

* **Handling the Button Phone Risk:** The AI registers the button phone held by Moyuri's mother-in-law as compromised. The system protocol enforces a "Zero Unsolicited Push" rule, meaning the DLAS will never independently initiate digital contact with that number.


* **The Friday 20-Minute Window:** The system meticulously records the 1:15 PM to 1:35 PM Friday window (during Jumu'ah prayer) as the singular digital safe zone for the DLAO to attempt direct contact.


* **Offline Covert Verification Mechanism:** Understanding that a 20-minute window is fragile, the AI's dossier recommends offline, covert verification. Under Section 14 and Section 16 of the Domestic Violence (Prevention and Protection) Act 2010, the DLAO can dispatch a female Upazila Women Affairs Protection Officer or a female Union Parishad member to Moyuri's residence. This visit can be executed under the guise of an innocent domestic health or welfare check, thereby verifying Moyuri's status and consent without alerting Sohel.



#### 3. Accessibility Accommodations for Visually Impaired Proxy

Ripon's visual impairment requires a radical departure from standard digital intake forms. The DLAS complies with the Legal Aid Services Policy 2014, Section 2(n), which mandates accessibility for persons with visual disabilities.

* **100% Voice Interface:** Because screen readers fail on Bengali text and Ripon cannot read PDFs or text fields, the entire intake loop is conducted via an interactive voice response (IVR) utilizing advanced conversational AI.


* **No Visual Bottlenecks:** The system entirely bypasses standard security bottlenecks like CAPTCHAs or SMS-based One-Time Passwords (OTPs), which would otherwise create a dead-end for a visually impaired user navigating a handed-down device.


* **Auditory Confirmation:** Instead of sending a case number via SMS, the system provides a highly memorable, short verbal reference token (e.g., a simple 4-digit spoken code) aligned with Ripon's auditory memory skills.

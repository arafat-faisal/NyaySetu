# Legal Tech Hackathon 2026 — Challenge 2: AI for Legal Aid
### Case: Moyuri Akter (Kalai, Joypurhat) — Proxy Intake via Ripon

## SECTION 1: OFFICIAL SUBMISSION RESPONSE (293 words)
*Ready to paste into the submission portal as-is.*

Ripon, blind since childhood, calls the National Legal Aid Helpline (16699) on behalf of his sister Moyuri, a domestic violence victim under extreme household surveillance in Kalai, Joypurhat. Because Moyuri never speaks to the system directly, the Digital Legal Aid System (DLAS) AI must function strictly as a voice-driven triage layer, not a decision-maker, consistent with the Legal Aid Services Act 2000 and Domestic Violence Act 2010 Section 16, which permits an authorised person to lodge a protection application on a victim's behalf.

**What AI can do:** Conduct fully voice-driven Bangla intake; extract structured entities (parties, allegations, dependents, location); classify Ripon's account explicitly as unverified third-party information; flag physical abuse, five months of economic deprivation, and dowry coercion under DV Act Sections 10–12 and Dowry Prohibition Act 2018 Sections 3–4; capture and lock the Friday midday safe-contact window; compile a structured dossier for the District Legal Aid Officer.

**What AI should not do:** Give substantive legal advice or predict outcomes; make any eligibility, acceptance, or rejection determination; reject the case for a missing NID or Moyuri's absence from the call; send any SMS, robocall, or notice to Moyuri's household or to Sohel; treat Ripon's account as established fact.

**When escalation occurs:** Immediately on detecting ongoing physical violence, child-affecting economic deprivation, dowry-linked threats of abandonment, or a surveillance situation preventing direct victim contact, all present here, mandating human handoff before any further action.

**Information for the human officer:** A dossier labelled "Unverified Third-Party Intake" containing Ripon's identity, relationship, and disability status; Moyuri's and her daughter's profile and location; the factual allegation narrative; an explicit no-outbound-contact warning; the Friday 1:15–1:35 PM safe window; a recommendation for covert verification via the Upazila Women Affairs Officer under DV Act Section 14; and statutory tags for DLAO review.

## SECTION 2: ARCHITECTURAL, SAFETY & STATUTORY RATIONALE

### 2.1 Decision Workflow — Step-by-Step Flow

```
STAGE 0  INBOUND CALL
         Ripon dials 16699. No visual UI, no menu tones requiring sight —
         a spoken Bangla greeting opens the call.
              |
              v
STAGE 1  ASR (AUTOMATIC SPEECH RECOGNITION)
         Bangla speech-to-text tuned for rural/regional accent variation
         and disfluent, emotional speech. Runs continuously; no "press 1"
         menus, since Ripon cannot see a keypad map and should not need one.
              |
              v
STAGE 2  NLU / ENTITY EXTRACTION
         Parses the narrative into structured fields:
           - Caller: Ripon (brother, visually impaired, phone number)
           - Subject: Moyuri Akter, ~24, Kalai, Joypurhat
           - Dependent: daughter, age 4
           - Respondent: Sohel (husband, rice-mill worker)
           - Allegations: nighttime battery; 5-month denial of maintenance;
             coercive dowry demand (motorcycle) with threat of bigamy
              |
              v
STAGE 3  SAFETY TRIAGE ENGINE
         Cross-checks extracted entities against escalation triggers
         (Section 2.4 of Attachment 3): physical violence, child-affecting
         destitution, dowry coercion, surveillance-blocked victim, caller
         distress. ANY single trigger = mandatory escalation. All five are
         present in this case.
              |
              v
STAGE 4  THIRD-PARTY / PROVENANCE FLAG
         Every field extracted from Ripon is stamped:
         "UNVERIFIED — THIRD-PARTY REPORT, PENDING DIRECT VICTIM CONTACT."
         This flag cannot be cleared by the AI under any condition; only a
         human officer, after confidential contact with Moyuri, may clear it.
              |
              v
STAGE 5  ANTI-SURVEILLANCE LOCK
         System captures the stated safe window (Friday, Sohel at Jumu'ah
         prayer, mother-in-law resting) and sets a hard rule in the case
         record: NO outbound SMS, IVR, or robocall to the household button
         phone, ever, under any workflow condition.
              |
              v
STAGE 6  DOSSIER COMPILATION
         Structured "Third-Party Intake Brief" assembled (see 2.4) and a
         verbal 4-digit reference token is read back to Ripon so he can
         confirm the case exists without needing to read anything.
              |
              v
STAGE 7  HUMAN HANDOFF — DISTRICT LEGAL AID OFFICER (DLAO)
         Case queued as URGENT. A human officer (or delegated Upazila
         Women Affairs Protection Officer) takes over all further contact,
         verification, and legal decision-making. AI involvement ends here.
```

The AI's entire function is compressed into Stages 1–6: listening, structuring, flagging risk, and locking down unsafe contact channels. Stage 7 — actual verification, judgment, and legal action — is exclusively human, matching the Evaluation Committee's rule that AI provides only primary support while case-management decisions remain human-centric.

### 2.2 Zero-Harm Anti-Surveillance Safeguards

| Risk Factor | Why It's Dangerous | System Safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Shared button phone controlled by mother-in-law; outgoing call logs inspected | Any inbound call, missed-call callback, or SMS is visible to the household and can trigger immediate retaliatory violence | Absolute ban on automated outbound contact to this number, hard-coded at the case level, not left to agent discretion |
| Sohel holds the only smartphone | Rules out any AI-initiated digital contact with the household entirely | No workflow branch assumes digital reachability of the victim |
| Friday ~20-minute window (Jumu'ah prayer + mother-in-law resting) | The one moment Moyuri can speak unsupervised | Captured verbatim as structured data ("Fri 1:15–1:35 PM") and passed to the DLAO as the *only* sanctioned direct-contact instruction, not a suggestion |
| NID locked in husband's almirah | Cannot be used as a verification credential; demanding it would stall or expose the case | AI is explicitly barred from treating missing NID as a rejection ground (Boundary 2.3, Attachment 3) |
| Need for confidential verification without alerting Sohel | A uniformed or obviously legal-aid visit would expose Moyuri to danger | Dossier recommends covert dispatch of a female Upazila Women Affairs Officer or female Union Parishad member under an innocuous "health/welfare visit" pretext, consistent with DV Act 2010 Section 14 (Protection Officer function) |

The unifying principle: the AI never initiates any channel of contact toward Moyuri's household. It only records constraints for a human to act on later, safely.

### 2.3 Accessibility Accommodations for the Visually Impaired Proxy

Ripon can dial and speak but cannot read forms, PDFs, captchas, or OTPs, and his phone's screen reader fails on Bangla labels. The DLAS design responds with:

- **Voice-only interaction end-to-end** — no menu requires sight; the AI speaks all prompts, confirmations, and next steps aloud in Bangla.
- **No OTP, no SMS confirmation, no app navigation** — identity and case continuity are handled through a **spoken 4-digit reference token**, repeated back and, if needed, re-read on a later call, rather than a code Ripon would have to read from a screen.
- **No forced structured answers** — the AI accepts free-flowing narrative speech and does the structuring itself (Stage 2), rather than asking Ripon to "select an option" he cannot see.
- **Graceful handling of what Ripon doesn't know** (e.g., Moyuri's NID) — the AI proceeds with intake regardless and flags the gap for the DLAO to resolve through Moyuri directly, rather than stalling on missing fields.

### 2.4 Statutory Alignment Matrix

| Case Fact | Statute & Section | AI Role | Human/DLAO Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nighttime beatings by Sohel | Domestic Violence (Prevention & Protection) Act 2010, §2(14) (physical abuse), §10 (Interim Protection Order) | Extract and tag as physical-abuse indicator; trigger urgent escalation | Assess evidence, seek Interim Protection Order from Magistrate |
| 5-month denial of food/maintenance for Moyuri and daughter | DV Act 2010, §2(14) (economic abuse), §12 (Monetary Relief & Maintenance) | Flag as economic abuse / child-endangerment trigger | Petition for maintenance and emergency monetary relief |
| Risk of eviction / disposal of the marital home | DV Act 2010, §11 (Residence Order) | Not applicable at intake (no stated eviction threat yet) — noted as a monitorable risk in the dossier | Assess need for a Residence Order if raised |
| Motorcycle dowry demand with threat of bigamy/abandonment | Dowry Prohibition Act 2018, §§3–4 (cognizable, non-bailable) | Extract and classify as dowry coercion | Determine whether to pursue criminal complaint |
| Application lodged by brother, not victim | DV Act 2010, §16 (application by an authorised person on victim's behalf) | Stamp all data as unverified third-party input under §16's representative-application framework | Independently confirm Moyuri's genuine condition and consent before any substantive order |
| Ripon's visual disability | Legal Aid Services Policy 2014, ¶2 (persons with disabilities qualify for free aid) | Note disability status in dossier as a qualifying/support factor | Confirm eligibility, arrange accessible follow-up channel for Ripon |
| Moyuri's insolvency / inability to travel 30 km | Legal Aid Services Act 2000; Legal Aid Services Policy 2014, ¶2 (insolvent women) | Note as eligibility-relevant context, not a determination | Confirm eligibility and waive in-person appearance where possible |
| Need for confidential field verification | DV Act 2010, §14 (Protection Officer) | Recommend covert verification channel in dossier | Dispatch Upazila Women Affairs Protection Officer / female UP member |
| Overall AI-human division | Legal Aid Services Act 2000 (DLAO statutory authority); CrPC/Civil Rules on representation | Intake, structuring, risk-flagging only | Sole authority over eligibility, verification, and case admission |

### 2.5 Why This Design Satisfies the Evaluation Committee's Rule

The AI never adjudicates, never contacts the household, and never treats Ripon's account as more than a flagged, provenance-stamped lead. Every legally consequential act — verifying Moyuri's consent, issuing notices, granting aid, and arranging safe contact — is reserved for the DLAO or a designated Protection Officer, in direct compliance with the mandate that *"AI may only provide relevant primary support, but human-centric intervention is required for final legal assistance and case-management decisions."*
